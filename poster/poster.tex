\sectiontitle{01}{The concept}
\textbf{Lexical ambiguity} means a word has several meanings.
\textbf{Context}, the surrounding words, helps identify the intended meaning.

\textbf{Homonymy: unrelated meanings} [4]. A river \textcolor{blue}{\textbf{bank}}
and a savings \textcolor{blue}{\textbf{bank}} refer to different things.
\textbf{Polysemy: related meanings} [4], such as ``newspaper'' for the
printed object or its publisher.

\textbf{Word embeddings} are vectors: lists of numbers representing words.
They are learned from language data, with similar word uses often producing
similar vectors.
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.477\textwidth}\vspace{0pt}\RaggedRight
\setlength{\parskip}{3mm}
\sectiontitle{02}{Static vs contextual}
\textbf{Static embeddings: one vector per word.}
GloVe learns from word co-occurrence counts [2]. Its vector for ``bank''
stays the same in river and money sentences. Both meanings share one vector.

\textbf{Contextual embeddings: one vector per use.}
BERT uses attention to combine information from surrounding words [3].
It builds a context-dependent vector for each occurrence of ``bank.''

\textbf{Both learn from context.} BERT also updates the target representation
for each new sentence.
\end{minipage}

\divider
\noindent
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.477\textwidth}\vspace{0pt}\RaggedRight
\setlength{\parskip}{3mm}
\sectiontitle{03}{Related approaches}
\textbf{Word sense disambiguation} selects a dictionary meaning.
Knowledge-based methods use definitions and word relations.
Supervised methods learn from sense-labelled examples [5].

\textbf{Word-in-Context (WiC)} instead asks whether two uses share a meaning [1].
We test pretrained vectors on this task.

\sectiontitle{04}{Hypothesis}
\textbf{BERT will distinguish word meanings more accurately than static GloVe.}
Sentence context should help separate meanings that share a fixed word vector.
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.477\textwidth}\vspace{0pt}\RaggedRight
\setlength{\parskip}{3mm}
\sectiontitle{05}{Methodology}
\textbf{1. Represent each use.} Compare GloVe target only,
GloVe nearby-word average and BERT target in context.

\textbf{2. Compare vectors.} Cosine similarity measures how closely their
directions align. Above a cutoff, predict ``same meaning.''

\textbf{3. Evaluate.} Select settings and cutoffs on \TrainCount{} training
pairs. Score \ValidationCount{} separate English validation pairs,
half same meaning and half different.

{\captionfont\textbf{Settings:} GloVe 6B, \GloveDimensions{} dimensions,
up to two words on each side. BERT-base-uncased, mean of target subwords
in the last four layers. No BERT fine-tuning.}
\end{minipage}

\sectiontitle{07}{Conclusion}
\textbf{Contextual word representations help distinguish meanings on WiC.}
BERT's \BertAccuracy{} accuracy supports our hypothesis.
Nearby-word GloVe reaches \ContextAccuracy{}. BERT still makes \BertErrorCount{}
errors, so context helps without resolving every ambiguous use.
